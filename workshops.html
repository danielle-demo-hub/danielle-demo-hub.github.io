Workshops  /  Small groups, hands on

Build it
in an hour.

Nobody trusts AI because they read about it. They trust it the first time it makes them something real.

I spend my days getting AI adopted inside institutions where being wrong is expensive. These sessions are the same problem at human scale: one room, one hour, and everybody leaves holding a finished thing instead of notes.

  • 60 minutes
  • No code required
  • Everyone ships

02  /  The two prompts

Thinking and building are different jobs.

Which is why there are two prompts and not one. The first runs in an ordinary chat and produces a brief. The second runs in Claude Code and produces the site. Collapse them into a single prompt and you get a confident, generic website about nobody in particular.

One  ·  in a Claude chat 
I'm building a one-page personal website for myself. Interview me.

Ask me questions one at a time until you understand what I want to be known for and what makes me different from everyone else with a similar job. Push back when my answers are generic — make me be specific. Ask about real numbers, real projects, and moments that actually happened.

When you have enough, write me a brief with:
- My name
- The one sentence I want to be known for
- Three specific proof points
- Two sentences about who I am
- Three words for how the site should feel

Start by asking me what I do.
Two  ·  in Claude Code 
Build me a personal website as a single file called index.html in this folder.

Here is my brief:

[PASTE THE WHOLE BRIEF CLAUDE WROTE FOR YOU IN THE CHAT]

CONTACT
[EMAIL] and [LINKEDIN URL]

REQUIREMENTS
- One page, one file. Everything inline. No separate CSS or JS files, and no images that live on my computer.
- Must work on a phone.
- Must work in light and dark mode.
- Two Google Fonts that suit the feeling above. Not Inter, not Space Grotesk.
- No stock photos, no clip-art icons, no emoji as decoration.
- My name and the one thing I want to be known for are the first things anyone reads.
- Give it a real point of view. I do not want something that looks like a template.

When you're done, tell me the full path of the file so I can open it.

Single file, everything inline, no local images — that line is what makes the publishing step work.

03  /  Six directions

The hard question isn't what your site should look like. It's which of these is you.

Six personal sites carrying exactly the same information — a name, one sentence, three proof points, a short bio, a way to get in touch — in six completely different voices. Pick one and describe it in words. That is a far easier question to answer than a blank page, and it is the whole reason people freeze at this step.

01

The Quiet One-Pager

Almost nothing on it. Restraint reads as seniority, and it is the hardest one to get wrong.

02

The Thesis

Leads with what you believe, then the proof. For being hired for how you think.

03

The Writer

An index of what you have published and nothing else. Needs three real things to list.

04

The Maker

Loud color, work in a grid, seen before it is read. Only if what you make is visual.

05

The Warm Introduction

Your face, your voice, one clear way to reach you. For when people must like you first.

06

The Résumé

Skimmable and chronological, built for someone hiring. The least expressive, the most useful.

04  /  After the hour

How to keep it alive.

The sites that still exist in a month are the ones whose owners know how to change them. It is four steps, and none of them involve a terminal.

Updating

Reopen, describe, re-upload

Open Claude Code on the same folder, say what you want changed, then upload the new index.html to GitHub over the old one. That is the entire maintenance story.

Your own domain

About $12 a year

A custom domain points at the exact site you already built. Nothing you make in the session gets thrown away when you upgrade.

Looking around

Where to browse

personalsit.es for real people's sites, minimal.gallery for calm ones, One Page Love for single-page only.

The rule

Reference, never copy

Describe what you like about a site in words — "lots of space," "big type," "dark and quiet" — and feed that into your brief. Never ask for a copy of someone's site.

05  /  Bring this to your team

An hour, and everyone has shipped something.

This format is not really about websites. It is about the moment a room stops treating AI as a thing to have opinions about and starts treating it as a tool they have personally used to finish something. That moment is what I get paid to engineer inside large institutions, and it turns out to work just as well around a kitchen table.

If you want a version of this for your team, your cohort, or your organization — or a different hour entirely — tell me who is in the room and what you would want them holding at the end.
